# Slow-pitch softball hitting: lower body (legs and feet), how it's coached and how it differs from baseball and fastpitch

Source-quality note (applies throughout): the strongest sources are the official rulebooks and two peer-reviewed biomechanics proceedings papers on *elite adult male slow-pitch hitters* (Wu, Gervais, Baudin & Bouffard, ISBS). Almost all "coaching" content I could reach is from bat retailers and blogs that name no author. The suggested elite-instructor sources (Smash It Sports, Bat Digest, Pocket Radar, YouTube channels, Softball Fans, r/Softball) either didn't show up in search or blocked fetching (403/404/503). I didn't pull YouTube transcripts because the only route (the vidIQ connector) spends paid credits. Where a claim comes only from a search-result excerpt of a page I couldn't open, it is marked **(excerpt only)**. Engineering thresholds in the "Inferences" sections are mine. They are not from a coach.

## 1. The pitch: arc rules, distance, speed, descent angle and time to the plate, and what that means for timing

### Takeaway
The legal arc differs by sanctioning body. USA Softball adult: 6–10 ft (12 ft for Seniors). NSA: 6–10 ft. SSUSA Senior: 6–12 ft. USSSA: at least 3 ft of rise after release and no higher than 10 ft (12 ft for Seniors, per a forum quote). The task brief's "6–12 ft for USA/ASA" holds only for USA *Senior* play. Measured slow pitches travel about 10–15 m/s (22–34 mph) and take about 1.5 s to arrive, roughly 3× the time a baseball or fastpitch hitter gets. My trajectory model puts the descent angle at the hitting height at about 18° (6-ft arc) up to about 40° (12-ft arc). A typical baseball fastball comes in at about 6°.

### Cited Findings
**Arc rules (heights are from the ground unless stated)**
- **USA Softball (2023):** "Delivery with an arc of not less than 6' and not more than 10' or 12' in Seniors". Fastpitch has "No regulation on the arc". Pitch must be "released with moderate speed". Pitching distance: "Women's, Men's, and Coed 50'"; 16-inch slow pitch is 38'. "Senior Slow Pitch has a strike mat". In Seniors the pitcher may be "up to 10 feet behind the pitcher's plate". "A step may or may not be taken". — [USA Softball 2023 SP vs FP rule differences](https://www.usasoftball.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/120/2023/06/2023-Rule-Differences_SPvsFP_Final-4.pdf)
- USA Softball rule wording: "perceptible arc and reach a height of at least 6 feet from the ground, while not exceeding a maximum height of 10 feet from the ground. Exception: Senior Slow Pitch Only shall not exceed a maximum height of 12 feet" **(excerpt only)** — [USA Softball DFW 2023 rulebook (issuu)](https://issuu.com/usasoftballdfw/docs/2023_rulebook_digital/s/17858314)
- **USSSA (2018 rulebook):** "The pitched ball must arc at least 3 feet after leaving the pitcher's hand and before it passes any part of home plate" and "shall not rise higher than 10 feet above the ground". A "flat level pitch" is an unfairly delivered pitch. Pitching distance is "50 feet for both men and women". — [USSSA 2018 Slow Pitch Rulebook](https://www.usssa.com/docs/2018/2018_SP_RuleBook.pdf)
- A 2025 officiating-forum post quotes the same USSSA 3-ft minimum and says a 12-ft maximum applies to Senior Slow Pitch only. A poster notes "You can release the ball from ankle height" under USSSA, unlike USA Softball **(excerpt only; thread wouldn't load)** — [Officiating.com forum, "USSSA v USASB slow pitch"](https://forum.officiating.com/softball/106436-usssa-v-usasb-slow-pitch-description-print.html)
- **NSA (2018 rulebook):** "The ball must be delivered with an arch and reach a height of at least 6 feet from the ground while not exceeding a maximum height of 10 feet from the ground". "The hand shall be below the hip". Adult men's and women's pitching distance is **53 feet**, not 50. Women's divisions use the 11-inch ball. — [2018 NSA Slow Pitch Rulebook](https://www.paducahky.gov/files/6/2018_NSA_Slow_Pitch_Rulebook.pdf)
- **SSUSA (2026 rulebook):** "The height of a legal pitch must be from six to 12 feet above the playing surface". The pitcher's box runs from the plate at 50 ft and extends back 10 ft. Strike-zone mat: 19 in wide × 34½ in long, aligned with the front edge of the plate. A pitched ball that hits the mat or ground "cannot be legally swung at". — [SSUSA Rulebook 2026](https://www.seniorsoftball.com/wc/wc2026/SSUSA-Rulebook-2026-Web.pdf)
- SSUSA umpires call an illegal pitch "in flight when ball reaches top of arc". Pitchers must "come set and pause". — [SSUSA 2026 Rules Summary](https://seniorsoftball.com/wc/wc2026/SSUSA%202026%20Rules%20Summary.pdf)
- **WBSC (international):** arc minimum 6 ft, maximum 12 ft **(excerpt only)** — [WBSC slow pitch page](https://www.wbsc.org/es/organisation/softball/slow-pitch-softball)

**Ball speed, flight time, research setup**
- "In a game of slo-pitch softball, the ball is pitched at a speed of 10-15 m/s and takes approximately 1.5 s to reach home plate". Baseball and fastpitch are "35-40 m/s and 20-25 m/s, respectively". The baseball/fastpitch batter "only has approximately 0.5 s". — [Wu et al., ISBS, stride and bat swing time in slo-pitch](https://ojs.ub.uni-konstanz.de/cpa/article/view/5606/5100)
- That lab simulated a game pitch at 14.44 m (50 ft minus the pitcher's ~0.80 m stride). Balls were thrown at "13.55 ± 0.77 m/s with an arc trajectory of 2.72 ± 0.22 m" (about 30 mph, apex about 8.9 ft). — [Wu et al., ISBS](https://ojs.ub.uni-konstanz.de/cpa/article/view/5606/5100); same setup in [Wu et al., ISBS 2011, ball field placement](https://ojs.ub.uni-konstanz.de/cpa/article/view/4793/4433)
- Because the ball is slow, "the batter has sufficient amount of time to use either an open, parallel, or closed stride". In baseball "the batter does not have as much time to adjust their stride technique as in slo-pitch". — [Wu et al. stride paper](https://ojs.ub.uni-konstanz.de/cpa/article/view/5606/5100); [Wu et al. placement paper](https://ojs.ub.uni-konstanz.de/cpa/article/view/4793/4433)
- Baseball comparison point: a FanGraphs swing analysis uses roughly a 6° descent angle for a typical MLB fastball **(excerpt only)** — [FanGraphs/THT "Optimizing the Swing"](https://tht.fangraphs.com/optimizing-the-swing/)
- I found no published slow-pitch descent-angle measurement. The only slow-pitch math I found is an idealized parabola exercise with no drag **(excerpt only)** — [surgent.net slowpitch](https://surgent.net/math/slowpitch)

### Inferences
- **Trajectory model (my own computation, not a published measurement).** Projectile with and without drag: 12-in ball, 0.19 kg, Cd 0.45. Release at 1.0 m (waist) and 14.4 m from the back of the plate, landing on the back tip of the plate. Run with Wu's measured release (13.55 m/s, 2.72 m apex), the model gives a 1.34 s flight that lands 15–16 m out, which agrees with Wu's "≈1.5 s". Results (with drag):

| Apex | Release speed | Flight time | Apex → ball at 0.8 m (belt/thigh) | Speed at contact | Descent angle at 0.8 m | Angle at the ground |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| USSSA-style flat (release 0.5 m, +3 ft rise) | ~37 mph | ~0.97 s | n/a | ~33 mph | ~14° | n/a |
| 6 ft | ~35 mph | 1.03 s | 0.46 s | ~31 mph | ~18° | ~24° |
| 8 ft | ~30 mph | 1.25 s | 0.59 s | ~27 mph | ~27° | ~32° |
| 10 ft | ~28 mph | 1.44 s | 0.69 s | ~25 mph | ~35° | ~39° |
| 12 ft (Senior max) | ~27.5 mph | 1.60 s | 0.77 s | ~25 mph | ~41° | ~45° |

- **What that means for timing.** Wu measured elite slow-pitch "bat swing time" (start of front-foot stride to contact) at 0.586 ± 0.144 s, with the front heel down about 0.21 s before contact. Set against the apex-to-contact window (0.46–0.77 s), a typical elite stride starts close to the top of the arc on a 6-ft pitch and roughly 0.1–0.2 s *after* the apex on a 10–12-ft pitch. The front foot lands while the ball is already coming down in every legal arc. This is the physics behind the folk cue "load as it's released, stride at the peak, foot down on the way down."
- **Steeper arcs shrink the hitting window.** At 10–12 ft the ball falls through the zone at 35–45°, so a swing plane that is slightly mistimed misses the ball vertically more than in baseball. That favors an upward-tilted bat path matched to the descent. This is the slow-pitch rationale for "getting under/behind" the ball (see Q3).
- **Rule profile for the app.** Ask for the league (USA / USSSA / NSA / SSUSA / rec). Defaults: arc range, distance (50 ft; NSA 53 ft; seniors effectively 50–60 ft because the pitcher may stand up to 10 ft back), and whether a mat is used (SSUSA/USA Senior: ball lands on a mat → very steep).

### Gaps
- I couldn't retrieve the current (2025–26) USSSA slow-pitch rulebook. The current-edition PDF link was a dead object, so the USSSA numbers are from the 2018 book plus a 2025 forum quote.
- No measured descent angles or release heights for real slow-pitch pitchers. Wu & Gervais (2008, *Sports Biomechanics* 7(1)) "An examination of slo-pitch pitching trajectories" appears to contain them, but I couldn't access it.
- The drag coefficient for a 12-in softball at 25–35 mph is assumed. The table could be off by a few degrees.

## 2. Timing approaches: load and stride relative to the arc; toe tap, leg kick, no-stride, walk-up/step-in/stutter; stance width and position in the box

### Takeaway
Coaches agree on one thing: load early and controlled (around the pitcher's release) so the slow arc doesn't make you load late, then let the ball come down. They split on whether to begin the swing at the apex or later, and on stride size. Moving "walk-up/stutter-step" approaches are used mainly by older players with roots in Chicago 16-inch and unlimited-arc ball. Top hitters reportedly don't use them. They are legal as long as foot-position-at-contact rules are met. Box position is arc-dependent: deeper in the box for high (USA) arcs, farther up for flatter (USSSA) arcs.

### Cited Findings
**When to load and stride**
- "As the pitcher releases the ball, shift your weight slightly onto your back leg. This is called the load." "In slowpitch, the arc can fool you into loading late. Either way, the load needs to happen before the pitch reaches you." "In slowpitch softball, patience means waiting on the arc and letting the ball get deep enough to drive." — [Bruce Bolt, How to Swing a Softball Bat](https://brucebolt.us/blogs/news/how-to-swing-a-softball-bat)
- "wait for the ball to reach its highest point in the arc before starting your swing" and "make contact with the ball as it begins to descend" — [Softball Ace, Hitting Farther in Slow Pitch](https://softballace.com/how-to-hit-a-softball-farther-in-slow-pitch/)
- "Keep your weight back, back, back, lift the front leg to start your stride" and "time it so your front foot goes down and your hands immediately come through". The named fault: "So many guys will lose their power because they stride, wait, and then swing." — [Pinetar Press, How to Hit a Slow Pitch Softball](https://pinetarpress.com/how-to-hit-a-slow-pitch-softball/)
- Elite data: front heel planted about 0.372 s after stride start (65% of the 0.586 s stride-to-contact time). The bat begins accelerating upward at about 0.493 s (85%). Peak bat speed is reached about 0.024 s before contact. — [Wu et al., ISBS](https://ojs.ub.uni-konstanz.de/cpa/article/view/5606/5100)

**Stride size (conflict)**
- *Big-stride camp:* "The key is to having a significant stride, not just some little baby step." — [Pinetar Press](https://pinetarpress.com/how-to-hit-a-slow-pitch-softball/)
- *Short-stride camp:* "take a short stride toward the pitcher… A long stride is one of the most common timing killers in softball. It shifts your weight forward too early and forces you to swing with your arms rather than your hips." — [Bruce Bolt](https://brucebolt.us/blogs/news/how-to-swing-a-softball-bat)
- The stride is a forward step with the front foot; "never step backward against a slow pitch". — [The Baseball Guide, Slowpitch Batting](https://www.thebaseballguide.com/slowpitch-batting/)

**Stance width and box position**
- "feet shoulder-width apart, knees slightly bent, and weight evenly distributed". To check distance from the plate, "extend your bat and tap the outside of the plate". "Whether you play fastpitch softball or slowpitch softball, the stance fundamentals stay the same." — [Bruce Bolt](https://brucebolt.us/blogs/news/how-to-swing-a-softball-bat)
- Shoulder-width, knees bent; one passage says weight on the balls of the feet, another says evenly distributed (the page contradicts itself). — [The Baseball Guide](https://www.thebaseballguide.com/slowpitch-batting/); [Softball Ace](https://softballace.com/how-to-hit-a-softball-farther-in-slow-pitch/)
- League-dependent depth: under ASA/USA the arc is higher so the ball enters the zone deeper, so stand farther back. Under USSSA the arc is lower and shallower, so "Position yourself closer to the pitcher." — [Pinetar Press](https://pinetarpress.com/how-to-hit-a-slow-pitch-softball/)
- In the Wu lab study, elite players "stood at their own self selected location in the batter's box with their own natural stance". — [Wu et al.](https://ojs.ub.uni-konstanz.de/cpa/article/view/5606/5100)

**Walk-up / stutter-step / moving approaches (SSUSA message board)**
- Users describe a "step-step-shuffle", a "two-to-four step approach", and "walking the box". It is "like a golfers waggle before bringing the club back". Some keep the back foot from passing the front foot; others cross over before the last step. It lets the hitter "stand back in the box in case ball came deep" and step up if the pitch is short. Origins are linked to Chicago 16-inch / unlimited-arc ball.
- Cons: "If you start to move you commit". It is hard to repeat ("my eye to ball angle changes a lot as I moved toward the ball") and harder in loose or uneven boxes. One poster: big-name slow-pitch hitters he watched don't use it. Claimed "10 to 15 feet" of extra distance is secondhand. — [SSUSA forum, "The Stutter Step"](https://seniorsoftball.com/?messageboard=&viewby_thread=103230&message_topic_id=misc)
- A senior slow-pitch instruction sheet describes "stepping forward with hands back until after the step and hip rotation" and says it is critical to get hip action into the swing **(excerpt only; PDF returned 503)** — [Super Senior Softball, "Three T's of Hitting"](http://www.superseniorsoftball.com/three_ts_of_hitting1.pdf)

**What the rules allow for moving approaches**
- USSSA: the box is only 5½ ft long ("extending 3 feet forward and 2-1/2 feet toward the rear" of the plate's center) × 3 ft wide. A ball is illegally batted when "one or both of his feet are in contact with the ground COMPLETELY outside the lines of the batter's box or when his foot is in contact with home plate". — [USSSA 2018](https://www.usssa.com/docs/2018/2018_SP_RuleBook.pdf)
- NSA: illegal if "A batter's entire foot is completely out of the box on the ground when he/she hits the ball" or "Any part of the batter's foot is touching home plate". — [NSA 2018](https://www.paducahky.gov/files/6/2018_NSA_Slow_Pitch_Rulebook.pdf)
- USA Softball: the only listed box difference from fastpitch is the 16-in slow-pitch box (4×8 ft); the fastpitch/standard box is 3×7 ft. — [USA Softball 2023 differences](https://www.usasoftball.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/120/2023/06/2023-Rule-Differences_SPvsFP_Final-4.pdf). USA language: illegal when "the batter's entire foot has contacted the ground out of the batter's box and returned to hit the ball while inside the box" **(excerpt only)** — [USA Softball DFW 2023 rulebook](https://issuu.com/usasoftballdfw/docs/2023_rulebook_digital/s/17858314)
- SSUSA: "At least some portion of both feet" must be on or inside the box lines "at the start of the pitch". The batter is out if he hits the ball "when his back foot is completely farther forward than the line defining the front edge of home plate. His front foot may be on the ground in front of the box when he hits the ball." Any part of a foot on the strike mat makes it an illegally batted ball. — [SSUSA Rulebook 2026](https://www.seniorsoftball.com/wc/wc2026/SSUSA-Rulebook-2026-Web.pdf); [SSUSA 2026 summary](https://seniorsoftball.com/wc/wc2026/SSUSA%202026%20Rules%20Summary.pdf)

### Inferences
- **Event timing for the analyzer.** Have the app detect (a) pitcher release (or ask the user to tap), (b) load onset (rear-hip shift / front-knee inward turn), (c) stride start (front ankle leaves its stance spot), (d) front-foot plant (front ankle settles, vertical velocity about 0), and (e) contact. Slow-pitch norm from the sources: load begins around release, and foot-down comes after the apex. From the elite data, plant to contact ≈ 0.2 s (the heel plant at 65% of a 0.586 s window). A plant-to-contact gap well over about 0.35–0.4 s is a candidate "stride, wait, then swing" flag. Plant after contact, or no plant at all, is "late/no foot down". These thresholds are mine and need tuning on real clips.
- **Don't apply baseball's "foot down early, before the ball is halfway" rule.** In slow pitch the ball is still rising or at the apex at the halfway point. Foot-down at the apex would leave 0.45–0.8 s of waiting (see the Q1 table).
- **Moving approaches must not be flagged as faults automatically.** Offer a "walk-up / stutter" mode that relaxes the "stance foot stays put" rule. In that mode, check box legality at contact against the league profile (rear foot not completely ahead of the plate's front edge in SSUSA; no foot planted completely outside a 5½-ft USSSA box). A phone can only estimate box lines if they are in view, so present this as advisory.
- **Stance width** advice in every source is generic shoulder-width. The app can measure stance width as ankle distance ÷ shoulder width and treat about 0.9–1.3 as "normal" (inference). Stride width is the measure that matters more for faults (Q4).

### Gaps
- I couldn't find any source with numbers for toe tap, leg kick or no-stride approaches in slow pitch, or for how often elite players use each. The Wu studies varied stride *direction* only.
- No slow-pitch source says whether a moving approach is legal "at the start of the pitch" under USSSA, USA or NSA. Only SSUSA explicitly requires both feet in the box at the start.
- One search excerpt gave a general benchmark of "front foot down about halfway" and "slightly after halfway for slower pitching". I couldn't confirm which page it came from, so I didn't use it.

## 3. Lower-body mechanics coached for power in slow pitch

### Takeaway
Slow-pitch sources repeat baseball's core model: load the back hip, short or controlled stride, plant and brace the front side, rotate hips before hands, legs as the power source. Three things are slow-pitch-specific. The arc leaves time to choose stride direction freely (no measured cost in elite players). Pull-side swings are measurably faster than opposite-field swings. And there is a running dispute between "chop/cut down on the bottom half" and "swing upward/slight uppercut" for lift. Sources also disagree on whether the front knee should be firm or straight versus kept bent, and on how far to "stay back."

### Cited Findings
**Back hip load and drive**
- "you must load your back hip for more torque and power". "Transfer your weight from the back hip to your front hip". "twist your hips on their axis" as the front foot lands. "Much of the explosive energy comes from the legs and up through the hips and abs." — [Pinetar Press](https://pinetarpress.com/how-to-hit-a-slow-pitch-softball/)
- "As your front foot plants after the stride, your hips should fire toward the pitcher before your hands move… Hips lead. Hands follow." "If your swing feels like it is powered by your upper body, power is being lost." — [Bruce Bolt](https://brucebolt.us/blogs/news/how-to-swing-a-softball-bat)
- Load cue: "rotate your front hip and knee back slightly toward the catcher as the pitch is on the way". Use "your front foot as a brace". **(excerpt only)** — [SportsRec, Improve Your Slow-Pitch Softball Swing](https://www.sportsrec.com/7322801/how-to-improve-your-slow-pitch-softball-swing/)
- "keep your front foot planted and pivot on your back foot" — [Softball Ace](https://softballace.com/how-to-hit-a-softball-farther-in-slow-pitch/)
- "a common mistake in slow-pitch softball is relying solely on upper body strength… the power for a strong hit comes from using your legs" **(excerpt only; the search tool didn't say which result it came from. The likely source is [Pelahatchie News, 4 Tips To Hit Better in Slow-Pitch Softball](https://www.pelahatchienews.com/article/2547,4-tips-to-hit-better-in-slow-pitch-softball), unverified)**

**Front leg: firm/straight vs bent (conflict)**
- *Firm/straight camp:* a firm front side is what the back leg and hips push against, and a soft front side "leaks" power **(excerpt only; general softball, not slow-pitch-specific)** — [Kuemper Softball, Batting Fundamentals](https://sites.google.com/a/kuemper.org/kuemper-softball/coachs-corner/batting-fundamentals). Base's Loaded's knee-to-ball drill says that at contact "the front leg should be straight". — [Base's Loaded Softball, drills to keep weight back](https://www.basesloadedsoftball.com/drills/softball-hitting-drills-to-keep-weight-back)
- *Bent camp:* a hitting checklist says to keep the front knee bent rather than locked, with the front hip initiating the drive **(excerpt only; generic)** — [Hitting Checklist (SportsEngine)](https://cdn1.sportngin.com/attachments/document/0116/9580/Hitting_Checklist.pdf)

**Stay back vs get off the back foot (conflict)**
- *Stay back:* "Avoid lunging forward too early; keep your hands back and let the ball travel before making contact." **(excerpt only)** — [JustBats, Slowpitch Softball Swing Tips](https://www.justbats.com/blog/post/slowpitch-softball-swing-tips) (page returned 403 to the fetcher)
- *Centered, not back:* hit "with your weight centered and your head between where your feet are planted" — [Base's Loaded](https://www.basesloadedsoftball.com/drills/softball-hitting-drills-to-keep-weight-back)
- *Dissent (fastpitch-oriented):* "You are actually not on the back foot when you hit it. Trying to stay on the back foot will cut the force on the ball." **(excerpt only)** — [Jugs Sports tip sheet](https://jugssports.com/content/Celeste/Jugs_Sports_Tip_Sheet_for_your_Players.pdf)

**Lift: "chop/cut" vs "upward" (conflict)**
- *Chop camp:* hitting with a chopping motion is "the secret to hitting a slow pitch softball", by cutting down through the bottom half of the ball. The same article lists launch-angle bands: home runs 20–40°, grounders <10°, line drives 10–25°, fly balls 25–50°, pop-ups >50°. — [Pinetar Press](https://pinetarpress.com/how-to-hit-a-slow-pitch-softball/)
- *Upward camp:* a downward swing is a "critical mistake"; "The swing should always be directed upwards." — [The Baseball Guide](https://www.thebaseballguide.com/slowpitch-batting/). Contact about a foot in front of the plate with "a slight uppercut" for backspin carry **(excerpt only)** — [SportsRec](https://www.sportsrec.com/7322801/how-to-improve-your-slow-pitch-softball-swing/). Lower-half rotation, not upper-body chopping, gives better contact control and bat speed; don't collapse onto the lower half **(excerpt only; page now 404)** — [International Softball blog](https://www.internationalsoftball.com/blog/how-to-hit-awesome-bombs-in-slow-pitch-softball/)
- Measured elite pattern: after front-heel plant "the bat began to accelerate upward" at about 85% of the swing time. Peak bat speed came 0.024 s before contact. — [Wu et al.](https://ojs.ub.uni-konstanz.de/cpa/article/view/5606/5100)

**Stride direction, pull vs opposite field**
- Elite slow-pitch hitters showed no significant difference in stride-to-contact time between open (stepping >30° toward the pull field), parallel (0.570 ± 0.144 s, fastest) and closed (>30° toward the opposite field) strides. "the batter may use whatever is most comfortable". — [Wu et al.](https://ojs.ub.uni-konstanz.de/cpa/article/view/5606/5100)
- Same-field (pull) hitting: success rate 48.34% vs 22.70% to the opposite field, and linear bat speed at contact 31.47 vs 29.62 m/s. This differed from a baseball placement study, and the authors attribute the difference to slow pitch allowing stride adjustment. Opposite-field hitters "let the ball come farther into the strike zone" (adjusting mainly through timing) **(the last point is excerpt only)**. — [Wu et al. 2011 placement](https://ojs.ub.uni-konstanz.de/cpa/article/view/4793/4433); [Human Kinetics, Improve Hitting Precision](https://us.humankinetics.com/blogs/excerpt/improve-hitting-precision)
- Sitting back slightly and keeping weight on the back side to drive outside pitches to the opposite field **(excerpt only)** — [Kingston Youth Lacrosse reprint, 11-inch slowpitch batting tips](https://kingstonyouthlacrosse.org/reviews/looking-to-improve-your-slowpitch-skills-15-must-know-tips-for-batting-with-11-inch-softballs.html) (low-quality source)

### Inferences
- **What "good" looks like in slow pitch (for scoring):** back-hip load visible before foot-down; stride lands before the bat starts forward; hips open before hands come forward; front leg firming (knee extending) from plant to contact; back heel up / back knee turning in toward the front knee by contact; head stays near the middle of the base. Each of these is measurable from 2D pose with sensible confidence from the side view.
- **Front-knee rule:** because sources conflict, score *direction of change* (front knee angle increasing, or not collapsing, between plant and contact) rather than a fixed "straight" angle. That satisfies both camps: "firm, not locked" and "straight at contact."
- **Stride-direction rule:** don't penalize open or closed strides by themselves (Wu found no timing cost). Do relate stride direction to the intended field: an open stride paired with a stated opposite-field goal, or a closed one with a pull goal, is worth a mild cue.
- **Lift:** given the 18–45° descent (Q1), the coach's "get under/behind the ball" maps to a bat path that rises through contact. The "chop" camp's actual effect is likely backspin from contact below center, but a downward bat path into a steeply falling ball is geometrically risky. The app should not coach "swing down." Lower-body proxy: rear-shoulder/rear-hip drop and a back knee that stays flexed (not collapsed) at contact.

### Gaps
- No slow-pitch-specific measurements of ground reaction force, weight distribution at contact, hip-shoulder separation or knee angles. The Wu lab published bat-only kinematics in the papers I could open. Its coordination paper (Crimson Publishers) wouldn't load.
- No named elite slow-pitch instructor (e.g., USSSA Conference/Major hitters) found in fetchable text. The coaching quotes above are mostly from anonymous blog or retailer authors.

## 4. Common slow-pitch lower-body faults: how they look (side view and from behind) and the cues used

### Takeaway
Sources name the same set of faults: lunging/drifting, overstriding, stride-wait-swing, upper-body-only swings, picking up or collapsing the back side, dropping the back shoulder (pop-ups) and rolling over (grounders). Visual descriptions are sparse. The best concrete check is "rear eye/head over the midpoint of the feet." I've added side-view and behind-view signatures below as pose-landmark inferences for the engineer.

### Cited Findings
- **Lunging:** shows as "too much bending in the front knee, especially if your knee moves over your toes", and the head ending in front of or behind a foot after the follow-through. It "will knock you off balance, and reduce the power of your swing." Cue: keep the head "completely centred" over a line between the feet. — [Base's Loaded](https://www.basesloadedsoftball.com/drills/softball-hitting-drills-to-keep-weight-back)
- **Lunging detection:** the rear eye should sit over the midpoint between the feet. "more than a couple of inches" (2 in) toward the front foot = lunging. Also check whether torso, front leg and front foot form a straight line at contact; if not, the upper body is lunging. Source is baseball/fastpitch oriented. — [Building Rome Series, Drills to Fix Lunging](https://buildingromeseries.com/?p=8668)
- **Back leg corkscrewing up:** the back leg swinging "forward and up" causes an "upwards corkscrew motion" of the hips. Fix: drive the back knee down toward the front leg. — [Base's Loaded](https://www.basesloadedsoftball.com/drills/softball-hitting-drills-to-keep-weight-back)
- **Overstriding:** a long stride "shifts your weight forward too early and forces you to swing with your arms rather than your hips". "shortening the stride is often the fastest fix." — [Bruce Bolt](https://brucebolt.us/blogs/news/how-to-swing-a-softball-bat)
- **Stride, wait, swing (disconnect):** "So many guys will lose their power because they stride, wait, and then swing." — [Pinetar Press](https://pinetarpress.com/how-to-hit-a-slow-pitch-softball/)
- **Loading late** (slow-pitch-specific): "the arc can fool you into loading late". — [Bruce Bolt](https://brucebolt.us/blogs/news/how-to-swing-a-softball-bat)
- **Upper-body-only swing:** "If your swing feels like it is powered by your upper body, power is being lost." — [Bruce Bolt](https://brucebolt.us/blogs/news/how-to-swing-a-softball-bat). Relying "solely on upper body strength" is a common slow-pitch mistake **(excerpt only; attribution unverified, likely [Pelahatchie News](https://www.pelahatchienews.com/article/2547,4-tips-to-hit-better-in-slow-pitch-softball))**
- **Swinging at everything early / rushing; poor balance; lunging; downward swing; poor footwork** are listed as slow-pitch mistakes without drills. — [The Baseball Guide](https://www.thebaseballguide.com/slowpitch-batting/)
- **Pop-ups:** dropping the back shoulder makes the bat drop below the hands; fix by keeping the shoulders level **(excerpt only, generic softball; the search tool didn't name the page, so the candidate [Hitting Faults and Fixes (SlideShare)](https://www.slideshare.net/sar84067/hitting-faultsandfixes114) is unverified)**. Pop-ups are launch angles >50°. — [Pinetar Press](https://pinetarpress.com/how-to-hit-a-slow-pitch-softball/)
- **Rolling over to weak grounders:** linked to a wrist roll at contact and to starting the swing with the hands instead of the hips **(excerpt only, generic; attribution unverified among results including [Correcting common hitting faults (TSHQ)](https://tshq.bluesombrero.com/portals/29505/docs/skill%20dev/hit/correcting_common_hitting_faults.pdf))**. Grounders are launch angles <10°. — [Pinetar Press](https://pinetarpress.com/how-to-hit-a-slow-pitch-softball/)
- **Picking up the back foot** is described as a common problem **(excerpt only; I couldn't attribute the exact page)**.

## 5. Differences for senior, women's/coed, and recreational vs competitive slow pitch

### Takeaway
Senior play (SSUSA, USA Senior) allows the highest arcs (up to 12 ft) onto a strike mat, so the ball falls about 40–45° and the hitter has the most time. Pitchers may stand up to 10 ft deeper, which changes timing from pitch to pitch. SSUSA rules let the front foot land outside the front of the box, and many seniors use stepping approaches. Women's play usually uses the same 50-ft distance (NSA: 53 ft), an 11-inch ball in some bodies, and shorter fences. I found no source on women-specific lower-body coaching. Recreational vs competitive differences in the rules show up mainly as home-run limits, which change whether lift or line drives is the right coaching goal.

### Cited Findings
- **Senior arcs and mat:** SSUSA arc 6–12 ft; 19 × 34½-in mat; balls hitting the mat cannot be legally swung at. — [SSUSA Rulebook 2026](https://www.seniorsoftball.com/wc/wc2026/SSUSA-Rulebook-2026-Web.pdf). USA Softball Seniors: max arc 12 ft, strike mat, pitcher "up to 10 feet behind the pitcher's plate". — [USA Softball 2023 differences](https://www.usasoftball.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/120/2023/06/2023-Rule-Differences_SPvsFP_Final-4.pdf)
- **SSUSA pitcher's box** is 2 ft wide × 10 ft deep; the pitcher keeps one foot touching it. — [SSUSA 2026 summary](https://seniorsoftball.com/wc/wc2026/SSUSA%202026%20Rules%20Summary.pdf). A forum poster mentions a then-new rule letting pitchers throw from anywhere in a bigger area. — [SSUSA forum](https://seniorsoftball.com/?messageboard=&viewby_thread=103230&message_topic_id=misc)
- **Senior batter's box:** front foot may be on the ground in front of the box at contact; back foot may not be completely past the front edge of the plate. — [SSUSA Rulebook 2026](https://www.seniorsoftball.com/wc/wc2026/SSUSA-Rulebook-2026-Web.pdf)
- **Senior count:** batter starts at 1-1 and gets one courtesy foul with two strikes. — [SSUSA 2026 summary](https://seniorsoftball.com/wc/wc2026/SSUSA%202026%20Rules%20Summary.pdf)
- **Senior home-run limits (50+):** Major Plus 9, Major 6, AAA 3, AA 1; Men's 40 Masters 10/8/6/3. Homers over the limit are outs. Older divisions field 11–12 defenders (Men's 65–80: 11; 85+: 12). — [SSUSA 2026 summary](https://seniorsoftball.com/wc/wc2026/SSUSA%202026%20Rules%20Summary.pdf)
- **Senior stepping approaches** (stutter/walk the box) are common among some seniors, with mixed reviews (see Q2). — [SSUSA forum](https://seniorsoftball.com/?messageboard=&viewby_thread=103230&message_topic_id=misc)
- **Women's/coed distances:** USA Softball 50 ft for women's, men's and coed; women's fences 265–300 ft vs men's 300–325 ft and coed 275–300 ft. — [USA Softball 2023 differences](https://www.usasoftball.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/120/2023/06/2023-Rule-Differences_SPvsFP_Final-4.pdf). USSSA: 50 ft for men and women; in coed, a walked male batter is awarded first and second, and the next female may choose to walk or bat. — [USSSA 2018](https://www.usssa.com/docs/2018/2018_SP_RuleBook.pdf). NSA women's divisions: 53 ft, 70-ft bases, 11-inch ball. — [NSA 2018](https://www.paducahky.gov/files/6/2018_NSA_Slow_Pitch_Rulebook.pdf)
- **Competitive vs rec in research:** the Wu studies used "skilled (class A/B division)" men, mean age 33.7, 12.7 years' experience. — [Wu et al. 2011](https://ojs.ub.uni-konstanz.de/cpa/article/view/4793/4433)
- Generic senior-adaptation advice (lighter bats, mobility-limited drills) appeared in search output only as the search tool's own suggestion, not from a source, so I excluded it.

### Inferences
- **Senior profile:** expect steeper descent (about 35–45°) and longer apex-to-contact time (about 0.7–0.8 s). Lower-body timing norms should shift later than in USSSA/men's play: foot-down comes well after the apex. Pitcher depth varies up to 10 ft, so the app should judge timing relative to the pitch's own apex, not a fixed time after release.
- **Senior mobility:** cues should favor shorter strides, less aggressive back-leg drive, and balance or finish checks over maximum hip-rotation speed. This is inference: I found no source on senior hitters' range of motion.
- **HR-limit-aware coaching:** in SSUSA AA/AAA (1–3 HR limits) or other limited leagues, the "lift for home runs" goal (20–40° launch) can cost outs. Offer a "line-drive goal" (10–25°) setting.
- **Women's/coed:** keep the same mechanics model, but use the league's ball size and distance in the timing model. Fence distance doesn't change lower-body mechanics.

### Gaps
- No sources found on differences in lower-body mechanics or coaching for women's or coed slow-pitch hitters.
- No sources comparing rec vs competitive hitters' lower-body mechanics (e.g., stride length, timing consistency).
- No research on senior slow-pitch hitting biomechanics.

## 6. Drills slow-pitch coaches use for each lower-body fault

### Takeaway
Fetchable drill descriptions are mostly generic softball/baseball drills that slow-pitch content also recommends. The best-specified ones target lunging (head-over-line, noodle, rear-eye test), back-leg action (knee-to-ball) and hip-first sequencing (hip turns without a bat). Slow-pitch blogs otherwise only list tee, soft toss, front toss and live pitching. I found no slow-pitch-specific drill for "stride at the apex" timing.

### Cited Findings
- **Knee-to-Head line drill (lunging):** draw a line between the feet; head must stay "completely centred" over it; look down after the follow-through to check. — [Base's Loaded](https://www.basesloadedsoftball.com/drills/softball-hitting-drills-to-keep-weight-back)
- **Knee-to-Ball drill (back leg corkscrew/collapse):** medicine ball between the legs; drive the back knee into it; at contact "your back knee should be touching the medicine ball" and the front leg straight. — [Base's Loaded](https://www.basesloadedsoftball.com/drills/softball-hitting-drills-to-keep-weight-back)
- **Rear-eye freeze test, noodle block, torso-line test (lunging):** freeze at contact and check the rear eye is within 2 in of the midpoint between the feet. A coach holds a pool noodle a stride-length in front of the helmet ("staying behind the noodle", 25 hard hits without touching it). Check the torso, front leg and front foot form one line. Progress from dry swings to tee to front toss. Baseball/fastpitch source. — [Building Rome Series](https://buildingromeseries.com/?p=8668)
- **Hip-rotation-without-bat (upper-body-only swing):** "Plant your front foot, drive your back hip forward, and feel the rotation in your core." — [Bruce Bolt](https://brucebolt.us/blogs/news/how-to-swing-a-softball-bat)
- **Short-stride fix (overstriding):** "shortening the stride is often the fastest fix". — [Bruce Bolt](https://brucebolt.us/blogs/news/how-to-swing-a-softball-bat). A no-stride swing drill was suggested for slow-pitch timing **(excerpt only; exact page unconfirmed)**.
- **General slow-pitch progression:** tee for mechanics, soft toss for timing and power, front toss for all-fields hitting, live pitching. — [Softball Ace](https://softballace.com/how-to-hit-a-softball-farther-in-slow-pitch/). Leg and core strength work rather than arm work for power. — [Pinetar Press](https://pinetarpress.com/how-to-hit-a-slow-pitch-softball/)
- **Rolling over / casting:** towel drill and hip-to-ball drill; soft toss with pause and "load and hold" drills for timing **(excerpt only, generic; attribution unverified, candidate [Correcting common hitting faults (TSHQ)](https://tshq.bluesombrero.com/portals/29505/docs/skill%20dev/hit/correcting_common_hitting_faults.pdf))**
- **Moving-approach practice:** posters say stutter-step hitting "takes a lot of practice" and was hard to repeat even off a tee. — [SSUSA forum](https://seniorsoftball.com/?messageboard=&viewby_thread=103230&message_topic_id=misc)

### Inferences
- **Mapping drills to app flags (suggested):** lunging → line drill / noodle / rear-eye test; overstride → short-stride or no-stride reps; back-leg collapse or corkscrew → knee-to-ball; spinning off or soft front knee → "plant and firm" (front-leg firming) reps off a tee; upper-body-only → bat-less hip turns; late foot down or stride-wait-swing → front-toss or live arc work with the cue "start the step at the top of the arc, foot down on the way down." The last is a slow-pitch arc drill I derived from the timing data in Q1–Q2. No source describes it.
- **Arc-specific practice:** soft toss and flat front toss don't reproduce the 18–45° descent. For slow-pitch timing drills the app should recommend real arc pitching (a partner lobbing within the league's arc range) where possible. This is an inference from the physics, not sourced.

### Gaps
- No slow-pitch-specific drills found for stepping in the bucket, heel-vs-toe landing, or popping up, and none from named elite slow-pitch instructors.
- I couldn't verify whether the elite-instructor YouTube content mentioned in the brief contains drills, because no transcripts were retrieved.
