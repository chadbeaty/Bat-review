# Lower-body hitting metrics: how commercial and lab systems define them, and published benchmark ranges

Scope: catalog of metric definitions (what is measured, from which landmarks/events, units) and reference ranges for lower-body hitting metrics, so a phone-camera implementation can mirror them with sensible thresholds. Baseball / fastpitch / slow-pitch are distinguished wherever the source allows.

Important note on the numbers in this file: besides published sources, this researcher downloaded Driveline's public OpenBiomechanics (OBP) hitting data (POI table + full-signal landmark and joint-velocity tables) and computed summary statistics directly. Those numbers are labeled **"OBP-derived (computed here)"** and cite the dataset as the source. The method for each derived metric is spelled out so an engineer can reproduce it. All OBP swings are baseball hitters (mostly college) hitting a pitching machine at ~65 mph from ~40 ft, so they are **not slow-pitch numbers**. OBP data is licensed for non-commercial use only (see Section 4). Check the licensing before using OBP-derived thresholds in a commercial product.

## 1. Which lower-body / pelvis metrics does each product report?

### Takeaway
Only the body-tracking systems (K-Vest IMU vest, Uplift Labs markerless video, Reboot Motion, lab mocap like Driveline/Qualisys, and Sportsbox AI for golf) report lower-body metrics: pelvis rotation angle and angular velocity, pelvis-torso separation (X-factor), kinematic/peak-speed sequence, pelvis bend/side-bend, lead knee angle, and center-of-mass/momentum. Bat sensors (Blast, Diamond Kinetics) and ball trackers (Rapsodo, HitTrax) report bat/ball metrics only. Their "timing" metrics (Time to Contact, Trigger to Impact) are the only ones that touch the lower-body timeline. No vendor found publishes slow-pitch-specific lower-body norms.

### Cited Findings

**K-Motion / K-Vest (IMU vest; pelvis, torso, lead arm, hand sensors)**
- K-Motion defines "peak speed sequence" as the order in which pelvis, torso, lead arm and hand/bat reach their peak angular velocities. "Rotation" is the degrees the pelvis and upper body rotate. "Separation" is created by rotating the upper body more than the pelvis, which "creates a stretch that is a source of speed". "Bend" is forward bend of the pelvis and upper body, and "side bend" is lateral tilt. — [K-Motion K-Baseball](https://www.k-motion.com/k-coach/k-baseball/); [K-Motion](https://www.k-motion.com/?p=10316)
- K-Motion: "if the pelvis is slow, then the torso or lead arm or hands have to work to catch up". — [K-Motion K-Baseball](https://www.k-motion.com/k-coach/k-baseball/)
- The K-Vest swing report is organized around events: Address, First Move, Heel Strike, Contact. Heel Strike is "the point when the front foot heel is firmly planted". First Move is the initial few inches of forward hand travel. One K-Vest write-up says Heel Strike is marked "when the Pelvis reaches an angular velocity of 100 degrees per second". Time to contact is "from the first move of the hands in a positive direction (towards the plate) to contact". Pelvic side-bend at first move is the side tilt of the pelvis toward the dominant side as rotation begins. (Attribution across these coaching pages came via search summaries, so treat the exact wording as secondary.) — [Rockland Peak Performance, K-Vest training](https://rocklandpeakperformance.com/?p=14522); [Rockland Peak Performance, 15 steps](https://rocklandpeakperformance.com/?p=16733); [Notre Dame Biomechanics in the Wild](https://sites.nd.edu/biomechanics-in-the-wild/2019/11/18/using-k-motion-technology-to-achieve-the-perfect-baseball-swing/)
- Driveline's OBP dataset publishes pelvis, torso and X-factor angles "using K-Vest conventions" at first move, foot plant and heel strike. This shows K-Vest's angle convention has become a de facto industry reference. — [OBP hitting README](https://github.com/drivelineresearch/openbiomechanics/tree/main/baseball_hitting)

**Uplift Labs (markerless multi-camera / phone video)**
- Uplift reports a kinematic sequence of pelvis, trunk and arm rotational velocities. The intended pattern is pelvis peak first, then trunk, then arm. Dashboards show the most common sequence and the distribution of sequences over a session. — [Uplift Labs help: Importance of the kinematic sequence](https://intercom.help/upliftlabs/en/articles/12480186-importance-of-the-kinematic-sequence) (seen via search snippet; the page returned 404 when fetched)
- Uplift has a help article titled "Hip-Shoulder Separation (X-Factor)". Its content could not be retrieved (404). — [Uplift Labs help](https://intercom.help/upliftlabs/en/articles/12480887-hip-shoulder-separation-x-factor)
- Uplift Capture app: "joint angles, rotational velocity, kinematic sequence, and key identified events in each movement". — [Uplift Capture, App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1501907981)

**Reboot Motion (markerless/mocap processing; momentum-based framework)**
- Pelvis rotation (`pelvis_rot`) is "the global rotation of the pelvis (as defined by a horizontal line between the hip joint centers) about the z axis". The zero is the player facing along +x for a right-hander and along −x for a left-hander. Pelvis side tilt (`pelvis_side`) is the tilt of the hip-joint-center line relative to horizontal. — [Reboot Motion Definitions](https://documentation-4dj.pages.dev/definitions)
- Hip flexion(+)/extension, adduction(+)/abduction, rotation around the long axis. Knee is "knee flexion angle with zero as full extension". Range metrics include `rear_hip_ir_range_norm` ("prior to the torso momentum peak") and `lead_knee_flex` ("before the torso momentum peak for hitting"). — [Reboot Motion Definitions](https://documentation-4dj.pages.dev/definitions)
- Segment momentum (mass × velocity) is reported for `lead_leg`, `rear_leg`, the lower half, and so on. Metrics include `lower_half_proj_max` (peak momentum in the bat direction) and side-to-side momentum (for hitters, "towards the first base dugout is positive"). — [Reboot Motion Definitions](https://documentation-4dj.pages.dev/definitions)
- Reboot's company writing says peak segment momentums, rotation-plane alignment and ranges of motion (e.g., hip-shoulder separation) correlated with performance. — [Reboot / Learn Your Keep: Measure What Matters](https://learnyourkeep.substack.com/p/measure-what-matters-correlation)

**Driveline (lab mocap, Launchpad-type reports; OBP open dataset)**
- The OBP hitting POI table includes pelvis angles at first move, foot plant and heel strike, plus pelvis angular velocity at first move, foot plant and max hip-shoulder separation, and maxima across phases. It also has torso-pelvis (X-factor) angles, lead knee at foot plant, max lead knee angle between load and foot plant, rear hip angles, and max center-of-gravity velocity. Full list in Section 4. — [OBP hitting README](https://github.com/drivelineresearch/openbiomechanics/tree/main/baseball_hitting)

**Qualisys PAF Baseball (lab mocap report template)**
- Batting report variables: pelvis rotation (pelvis wrt lab, "Fwd +"), pelvis angular velocity, lead/back hip, knee and ankle angles and velocities, stride length (defined only in a figure), knee and back-foot positions "toward home plate", pelvis and trunk COG velocity magnitude, and lead/back leg GRF normalized to bodyweight. — [Qualisys PAF Baseball, Appendix A](https://docs.qualisys.com/baseball/Content/PAF%20Baseball/Appendix%20A%20-%20variable%20definitions.htm)

**4D Motion (IMU)**
- No 4D Motion hitting metric documentation was found. A validity study (pitching) found it "should not be considered equivalent" to marker-based capture: it over-reported most metrics, and angular velocities were the most consistent. Pelvis rotation velocity was among the metrics tested. — [Are 4D Motion Sensors Valid and Reliable for Studying Baseball Pitching? (record)](https://katalog.lib.cas.cz/KNAV/EdsRecord/cmedm,37067847)

**Blast Motion (bat sensor; Blast Baseball and Blast Softball apps)**
- Softball metrics on Blast's softball mechanics page: Bat Speed ("top-end speed of the barrel through the zone"), Rotational Acceleration, On-Plane Efficiency (target ≥70%), Attack Angle (most fastpitch benchmarks 0°–15°), Early Connection (torso to lead-arm angle at start of rotation; target 90°, healthy 80–105°), Connection at Impact, Time to Contact, and Power. Lower body appears only as coaching cues ("land firm, turn behind your front hip", "back pocket down the line"). There are no hip or stride numbers. — [Blast: Softball Swing Mechanics](https://blastmotion.com/blog/softball-swing-mechanics/)
- Blast's softball benchmarks "are measured on fastpitch hitters". The page mentions no slowpitch mode or slowpitch norms. — [Blast: Softball Swing Mechanics](https://blastmotion.com/blog/softball-swing-mechanics/)
- The Blast Softball app overlays bat speed, time to contact and attack angle on video and estimates ball flight from video. No slowpitch setting was documented in the listing. — [Blast Softball, App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1228437292)

**Diamond Kinetics (bat sensor)**
- 11 metrics: Hand Speed, Max Barrel Speed (Speed); Trigger to Impact (Quickness); Potential Exit Distance, Applied Power, Max Acceleration, Impact Momentum (Power); Vertical Bat Angle, Hand Cast Distance, Distance in the Zone, Attack Angle (Control). No lower-body metrics. — [Diamond Kinetics help: Swing Metrics](https://help.diamondkinetics.com/hc/en-us/articles/39580729086359-Swing-Metrics)
- Trigger to Impact: ms "from the start of your forward bat motion to the moment of impact" ("from the instant the player begins accelerating the bat toward the ball"). — [DK Bat Sensor](https://diamondkinetics.com/bat-sensor)

**Rapsodo Hitting, HitTrax**
- HitTrax output (as exported in OBP) is ball-flight only: pitch speed, strike-zone region, launch angle, distance, result, spray bearing, and point of impact. — [OBP hitting README (hittrax.csv schema)](https://github.com/drivelineresearch/openbiomechanics/tree/main/baseball_hitting)
- Rapsodo Hitting documentation was not retrieved in this session (see Gaps).

**Mustard, OnForm/Coach's Eye**
- Mustard's public materials center on pitching and golf (AI 3D motion analysis, 1–10 grades for golf). No hitting-specific lower-body metric list was found. OnForm offers generic markup (lines/angles), slow motion up to 240 fps, and side-by-side/overlay comparison. No defined hitting metrics. — [Mustard: Pitching, App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id1526812408); [Golf Digest on Mustard](https://www.golfdigest.com/story/a-new-way-to-get-better-mustard-swing); [OnForm, App Store](https://apps.apple.com/US/app/id1490334045)

**Sportsbox AI (golf; single-phone 3D) — lower-body analogs**
- Pelvis Sway is lateral hip movement in inches, with address = 0". Per GOLF.com's Sportsbox coverage, most golfers (e.g., Rory McIlroy) sway their hips toward the target "up to six inches by impact". — [GOLF.com Sportsbox article](https://golf.com/instruction/sportsbox-ai-exposed-flaws-golf-swing-play-smart/); [Golf 24/7 podcast on Sportsbox sway data](https://poddtoppen.se/podcast/1795740895/golf-247eu-the-global-platform-for-innovative-technologies-and-teaching-concepts/232-using-sway-data-to-detect-5-golf-swing-faults)
- Pelvis Turn is in degrees, negative = turned away from target. A Golf Digest chart cites pros at about −40° pelvis turn at the top versus about −100° shoulder turn. — [Golf Digest: Sportsbox swing facts](https://www.golfdigest.com/story/useful-golf-swingfacts-sportsbox-ai-graph)
- Pelvis Lift is a "proxy of how well you are generating vertical force". The best players drop the pelvis in the downswing, then lift it before impact. — [GOLF.com: pelvic lift](https://golf.com/instruction/driving/pelvic-lift-generate-power-sportsbox-ai/)
- Penn State golf biomechanics notes that the start of pelvis turn is defined differently across 3D platforms (e.g., V3D/AMM/Kwon3D vs Golf BioDynamics). — [Penn State Golf: Rotation](https://golf.psu.edu/interactives/rotation)

### Inferences
- The most common set of lower-body hitting metrics across body-tracking vendors is: (1) peak pelvis axial angular velocity (deg/s), (2) pelvis rotation angle at key events (first move / heel strike or foot plant / contact), (3) torso-pelvis separation at foot plant and its maximum, (4) kinematic sequence order and peak timing, (5) lead-knee angle at foot plant and its change to contact, (6) COM / pelvis linear travel (weight shift, sway). A phone app that offers these six covers what K-Vest, Uplift and Reboot show users.
- Golf's sway / thrust / lift triad (pelvis-center displacement from address in three axes, in inches) maps directly onto hitting "weight shift / drift / sink". It is the most camera-friendly lower-body family because it needs only a midpoint of the two hip landmarks plus a scale reference.
- Bat-sensor timing metrics (Blast TTC, DK Trigger to Impact) start at bat or hand movement, not at a lower-body event. A camera app that times "foot down → contact" measures a different interval and should not borrow their benchmarks.

### Gaps
- Rapsodo Hitting and HitTrax product docs were not fetched. Based on the HitTrax export schema they appear to be ball-flight only, but this was not confirmed from vendor pages.
- Uplift Labs' X-factor and other metric pages returned 404. Exact Uplift definitions (event detection, separation landmarks) are unconfirmed.
- No official K-Vest glossary or definitions PDF was retrievable. K-Vest definitions come from K-Motion marketing pages plus coach write-ups.
- No official Sportsbox AI glossary was found. Exact thrust/lift units and whether sway uses the pelvis center or hip joint centers are unconfirmed. Penn State's linear-motions page (sway/thrust/lift definitions) returned 403.
- 4D Motion hitting outputs: no documentation found.
- No source documented a "slowpitch" mode in Blast's apps, so it is unknown whether one exists or what it changes.

## 2. Exact definitions: events, normalization, zero references, separation, lead-leg block

### Takeaway
Definitions differ in ways that matter. "Foot plant" ranges from first touch (10 N, or 10% bodyweight) to full weight acceptance (100% bodyweight), and sometimes a pelvis-velocity threshold stands in for it. Pelvis zero is usually "hip line parallel to the pitcher–catcher line (square to the plate)", with open toward the pitcher positive. But Driveline's raw convention is offset by 90° from the K-Vest convention. No vendor publishes a hitting stride-length normalization. Lab practice measures ankle-to-ankle or lead-ankle displacement in meters, and pitching uses % body height. "Lead leg block" has no standardized hitting metric; the closest are lead-knee extension (degrees, foot contact → contact), knee extension velocity, and lead-leg GRF.

### Cited Findings

**Foot contact / foot plant / heel strike events**
- Driveline OBP: "front foot contact (10% bodyweight)" and "front foot plant (100% bodyweight)", both on the front force plate, plus "contact". Hitters start with one or both feet on the back plate and land with only the lead leg on the front plate. — [OBP hitting README](https://github.com/drivelineresearch/openbiomechanics/tree/main/baseball_hitting)
- Qualisys PAF batting: without force plates, Lead_Foot_Off = toe marker rises 0.07 m above ground (shifted −0.04 s). Lead_Foot_Down = global minimum of the vertical lead-toe velocity, shifted 0.1 s forward. With force data, both use a 10 N GRF crossing. Contact, if not set manually, is maximum bat angular velocity. Setup = vertical speed of the opposite heel marker exceeds 0.1 m/s. — [Qualisys PAF Baseball, Appendix A](https://docs.qualisys.com/baseball/Content/PAF%20Baseball/Appendix%20A%20-%20variable%20definitions.htm)
- Reboot Motion: `max_stride` = "approximate time of foot contact, when stride length is at its peak and lead foot has moved downward". Max stride is "the moment when the player's lead leg reaches its max stride length while also being below a vertical threshold". Normalized time runs 0 = first increase in momentum toward the field to 100 = peak body momentum. A segment "starts" when it passes 5% of its max. Contact comes from Hawk-Eye (`bat_contact_hawkeye`). — [Reboot Motion Definitions](https://documentation-4dj.pages.dev/definitions)
- K-Vest (coach write-ups): Heel Strike = front heel firmly planted. Operationally, one write-up marks it when pelvis angular velocity reaches 100 deg/s. — [Rockland Peak Performance](https://rocklandpeakperformance.com/?p=14522); [Notre Dame Biomechanics in the Wild](https://sites.nd.edu/biomechanics-in-the-wild/2019/11/18/using-k-motion-technology-to-achieve-the-perfect-baseball-swing/)
- OBP-derived (computed here): pelvis axial velocity first exceeded 100 deg/s a median **42 ms before** the 10%-BW front-foot contact (IQR −58 to −20 ms; 10th–90th pct −81 to +79 ms; n = 647 swings). A velocity-threshold "heel strike" therefore usually fires before real ground contact and is highly variable. — computed from [OBP hitting full-signal release (landmarks, joint_velos)](https://github.com/drivelineresearch/openbiomechanics/releases/tag/dataset-v1)
- OBP-derived (computed here): 10%-BW contact → 100%-BW plant took a median 44 ms (IQR 36–67 ms; right-skewed, mean 78 ms). — [OBP release dataset-v1](https://github.com/drivelineresearch/openbiomechanics/releases/tag/dataset-v1)

**Swing start / time-to-contact start**
- Blast Time to Contact: "the elapsed time between the start of the downswing and the moment of impact". The algorithm detects when "functional forward bat speed" starts, so waggle and pre-swing movement don't start the clock. — [Blast: Time to Contact in Softball](https://blastmotion.com/blog/what-is-time-to-contact-in-softball-hitting/)
- K-Vest time to contact: first forward hand movement to contact. — [Rockland Peak Performance](https://rocklandpeakperformance.com/?p=16733)
- Diamond Kinetics Trigger to Impact: start of forward bat acceleration to impact (ms). — [DK Bat Sensor](https://diamondkinetics.com/bat-sensor)

**Pelvis rotation zero and sign**
- Reboot: hip-joint-center line about vertical; zero = right-hander facing +x (the hitting Y axis points from plate to rubber). — [Reboot Motion Definitions](https://documentation-4dj.pages.dev/definitions)
- Driveline OBP raw convention: pelvis Z = "Axial Rotation Toward (+)/Away (−) From Mound". Global frame +x plate → mound, +y toward the right-handed batter's box, +z up. Joint angles were adjusted for "righty/lefty symmetry, sign negations, adding or subtracting 90 degrees". — [OBP hitting README](https://github.com/drivelineresearch/openbiomechanics/tree/main/baseball_hitting)
- OBP-derived (computed here): OBP's "K-Vest convention" pelvis angle equals the raw Driveline angle **+90°** in every case checked (first move −21.3° vs −111.3°; foot plant +20.6° vs −69.4°). So K-Vest zero = pelvis square to the plate (hip line pointing at the pitcher), closed negative and open positive. — [OBP poi_metrics.csv](https://github.com/drivelineresearch/openbiomechanics/blob/main/baseball_hitting/data/poi/poi_metrics.csv)
- Pitching analog: pelvis rotation from left/right ASIS markers is 90° when the pelvis faces the x-axis (perpendicular to home plate) and 0° facing home plate. That is a different zero from the hitting convention. — [Pelvis position at foot contact in pro pitchers, OJSM 2022 (PMC9720809)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9720809)
- Qualisys: pelvis rotation wrt virtual lab, "Fwd +". X and Z components "often do not yield reasonable values due to gimbal lock". — [Qualisys PAF Baseball, Appendix A](https://docs.qualisys.com/baseball/Content/PAF%20Baseball/Appendix%20A%20-%20variable%20definitions.htm)

**Separation / X-factor**
- OBP: "x-factor angle … (same as torso-pelvis angle) – using K-Vest conventions" at first move, foot plant and heel strike. Also maxima of torso-pelvis angle between load and foot plant and between load and contact. Sign: torso rotated toward catcher relative to pelvis = negative (in the K-Vest-convention x_factor variables). — [OBP hitting README](https://github.com/drivelineresearch/openbiomechanics/tree/main/baseball_hitting)
- K-Motion: separation = upper body rotating more than the pelvis. — [K-Motion K-Baseball](https://www.k-motion.com/k-coach/k-baseball/)
- Reboot: hip-shoulder separation is treated as thoracic spine rotation range of motion between the lower half and lead arm, computed after the rear-leg momentum peak. — [Reboot Motion Definitions](https://documentation-4dj.pages.dev/definitions)
- OBP-derived (computed here): a camera-style separation, the transverse-plane angle between the **shoulder-joint-center line** and the **hip-joint-center line**, peaks at a median **39.4°** (IQR 33.3–45.3°). OBP's thorax-segment-based torso-pelvis maximum is a median **20.6°** (IQR 16.2–25.5°) on the same swings. Shoulder-landmark separation therefore roughly doubles true trunk-pelvis separation, because the shoulder joint centers move with the scapulae and arms. — [OBP release dataset-v1](https://github.com/drivelineresearch/openbiomechanics/releases/tag/dataset-v1); [OBP poi_metrics.csv](https://github.com/drivelineresearch/openbiomechanics/blob/main/baseball_hitting/data/poi/poi_metrics.csv)

**Stride length and direction**
- Qualisys: stride length is defined only in a figure. Positions are measured "toward home plate". — [Qualisys PAF Baseball, Appendix A](https://docs.qualisys.com/baseball/Content/PAF%20Baseball/Appendix%20A%20-%20variable%20definitions.htm)
- OBP POI has no stride-length variable. Stride can be computed from the ankle joint centers (`lajc`, `rajc`) in the landmarks table. — [OBP hitting README](https://github.com/drivelineresearch/openbiomechanics/tree/main/baseball_hitting)
- Pitching analog normalization: stride = medial-malleolus distance at foot contact, expressed as % body height (%BH). — [OJSM 2022 (PMC9720809)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9720809)
- Slow-pitch: Wu et al. had players use "open, parallel or closed" stride techniques to place the ball (same vs opposite field). Stride direction is a deliberate tactical variable in slow-pitch. — [Wu, Gervais, Baudin & Bouffard, ISBS 2011](https://ojs.ub.uni-konstanz.de/cpa/article/view/4793/4433)

**Lead leg / "block"**
- Reboot: lead knee flexion with zero = full extension. The range metric covers the period before the torso momentum peak. — [Reboot Motion Definitions](https://documentation-4dj.pages.dev/definitions)
- Pitching analog: "Lead knee extension" = total angular displacement from foot contact to ball release. Peak lead-knee extension velocity is reported in °/s. — [OJSM 2022 (PMC9720809)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9720809)
- Hitting: lead-foot peak vertical, posterior and resultant GRFs were all correlated with bat speed (Orishimo et al., Sports Biomechanics 2024). — [Mount Sinai Scholars: Lower extremity factors associated with bat speed](https://scholars.mssm.edu/en/publications/lower-extremity-kinematic-and-kinetic-factors-associated-with-bat/)
- Welch et al. 1995: front-foot force equal to 123% of bodyweight "promotes segment acceleration around the axis of the trunk". — [Welch et al. 1995, MEDLINE abstract](https://pesquisa.bvsalud.org/sms/resource/pt/mdl-8580946)
- A non-peer-reviewed OBP analysis frames "lead leg block" as rapid lead-knee extension after foot strike that converts forward momentum into rotation, and labels it a hypothesis. — [DEV Community OBP analysis](https://dev.to/yasumorishima/does-exit-velocity-come-from-the-front-foot-exploring-driveline-motion-capture-data-4d40)

**Weight shift / COM**
- OBP: `max_cog_velo_x` = "maximum center of gravity velocity from start of take to end of take (meters per second)". The landmarks table includes `centerofmass_x/y/z`. — [OBP hitting README](https://github.com/drivelineresearch/openbiomechanics/tree/main/baseball_hitting)
- Reboot: side-to-side momentum (balance) and segment momentum toward the field. — [Reboot Motion Definitions](https://documentation-4dj.pages.dev/definitions)
- Golf analog: Sportsbox pelvis sway = lateral pelvis travel from address (0") in inches. — [GOLF.com Sportsbox article](https://golf.com/instruction/sportsbox-ai-exposed-flaws-golf-swing-play-smart/)

**Drill condition affects definitions**
- Hitters show a more closed pelvis and trunk off a tee than in front toss. — [WKU IJESAB abstract: pelvis and trunk differences tee vs front toss](https://digitalcommons.wku.edu/ijesab/vol16/iss1/202)

### Inferences
- For a phone camera, use two foot events: **touchdown** (lead-ankle/heel vertical velocity changes from downward to near zero, or lead-heel height returns to its stance baseline ± tolerance) and **foot flat / weight accepted** (lead ankle stationary plus pelvis decelerating forward). Expect touchdown → weight-accepted gaps of ~30–70 ms (median 44 ms in OBP). At 30 fps that is only 1–2 frames, so these events can't be told apart at 30 fps.
- Do not define heel strike from a pelvis-velocity threshold. In OBP it precedes real contact by ~40 ms median and varies by ±100 ms.
- Recommended pelvis zero for the app: hip line pointing at the pitcher (square), open positive, mirrored for left-handers. This matches K-Vest/OBP conventions, so users can compare to K-Vest-style numbers. From a single side or front camera, the transverse-plane angle must be estimated from depth-ambiguous 2D landmarks. MediaPipe's z-coordinate, or the apparent hip-width foreshortening, is the only cue.
- Separation computed from shoulder and hip landmarks (as MediaPipe gives) should **not** be compared with K-Vest/OBP X-factor numbers. It runs about 2× larger (≈40° vs ≈21° peak). Either label it "shoulder-hip separation (camera)" with its own thresholds or apply an empirical scale.
- Stride normalization options, in order of robustness for a camera: (a) ratio to the hitter's own initial stance width (unitless, needs no calibration); (b) % of standing height (needs a height entry, or calibration from the hitter's own pixel height in a set-up frame); (c) % of leg length (hip-knee-ankle segment sum from landmarks).

### Gaps
- No vendor-published normalization for hitting stride length (body height vs leg length vs stance width) was found. Qualisys shows its definition only as an image.
- No vendor-published quantitative "lead leg block" metric for hitting was found.
- The exact K-Vest axis definitions (Euler order, whether "rotation" is global or relative to the address frame) are unconfirmed. Only the +90° offset relative to Driveline's raw convention was verified from data.
- OBP does not define its "first move", "heel strike", "load" or "loaded position" events in the README, even though variables use them.

## 3. Benchmark ranges: pelvis velocity, stride, timing, sequencing, lead knee, weight shift, and softball/slow-pitch

### Takeaway
For skilled baseball hitters, peak pelvis axial angular velocity is ~600–800 deg/s (Welch 1995: 714 deg/s; OBP college/pro: 705 ± 75 deg/s), and the torso peaks ~900–950 deg/s. The pelvis peaks ~75 ms before contact and only a few ms before the torso. The lead foot lands ~175 ms before contact against a 65 mph machine. Stride at foot contact is ~50% of body height ankle-to-ankle (~1.3× stance width), and normalized stride is not related to bat speed. The lead knee goes from ~46° flexion at foot contact to ~16° at contact (~30° extension). The center of mass drifts back ~7% of height during the load and finishes ~12–13% of height forward. Slow-pitch has almost no published lower-body data. The only quantified slow-pitch kinematics found are bat speeds (~30–31 m/s ≈ 66–70 mph in elite male slow-pitch) and pitch timing (~1.5 s ball flight).

### Cited Findings

**Pelvis peak angular velocity**
- Welch et al. 1995 (pro hitters, 3D): hip segment max 714 deg/s, then shoulders 937 deg/s, giving 31 m/s (~69 mph) bat speed. — [Welch et al. 1995, MEDLINE abstract](https://pesquisa.bvsalud.org/sms/resource/pt/mdl-8580946)
- OBP-derived (computed here; 677 swings, 98 hitters, mostly college plus some MiLB/independent/HS): `pelvis_angular_velocity_seq_max_x` mean **705 ± 75 deg/s**; 10th/25th/50th/75th/90th pct = 602 / 660 / 714 / 754 / 797 deg/s. Torso peak 915 ± 80 deg/s (806 / 862 / 920 / 963 / 1011). — [OBP poi_metrics.csv](https://github.com/drivelineresearch/openbiomechanics/blob/main/baseball_hitting/data/poi/poi_metrics.csv)
- OBP-derived by level (swing means): college 704, high school 701, independent 733, MiLB 707 deg/s. ### Inferences
Thresholds suggested for a phone app. These are inferences from the cited baseball data, not published slow-pitch norms.
- **Pelvis peak rotation speed:** "typical skilled" 600–800 deg/s, "elite" >750, "low" <550. Only meaningful at ≥120–240 fps. At 30 fps the pelvis turns ~20° per frame near peak, so peak speed is badly under-sampled. A 2D hip-line estimate ran ~4% higher than the 3D value in OBP (734 vs 707 deg/s). Because the pelvis peak barely separates bat-speed groups (r ≈ 0.15), it should be shown as context, not a score.
- **Pelvis at contact (open):** typical 75–105° from square. "Pelvis still closed/under-rotated" if <60°. Pelvis at foot contact typically −25° to 0° (still closed). "Opened early" if >+10° at touchdown.
- **Load coil (stance → max closed):** typical 10–35°. ~0° = no hip load.
- **Stride (ankle-to-ankle at touchdown):** typical 43–56% of height ≈ 1.1–1.7× stance width. Because normalized stride doesn't predict bat speed, flag only extremes (e.g., <1.0× stance width = "no stride/spin", >2.0× = "overstride"). Slow-pitch hitters who use a deliberate open/closed step may sit outside baseball ranges, so present it as descriptive.
- **Stride direction / foot line at touchdown:** typical −4° to +10° (slightly closed). Large open values (>15–20° toward the pull side) = "stepping in the bucket", unless the user selects an intentional placement (opposite-field) mode, which slow-pitch uses tactically.
- **Lead-leg block:** knee extension from touchdown to contact typical 20–40°, lead knee at contact typical 5–25° flexion. "Soft front leg" if extension <10° or knee >35° at contact. This is the lower-body metric with the clearest (still modest) link to bat speed and exit velocity in OBP (athlete r ≈ 0.27–0.28).
- **Weight shift:** COM/pelvis-midpoint forward travel *after* touchdown should be small (pelvis drift ≈ 0–5% of height). Large post-landing drift (>8%) is "lunging/drifting". Forward travel before landing (~2–10% of height) is normal.
- **Touchdown → contact timing:** baseball median ~175 ms against 65 mph. Slow-pitch has ~3× more ball-flight time, but no source quantifies slow-pitch touchdown-to-contact. The app should record its own slow-pitch distribution rather than borrow the baseball value. At 30 fps, 175 ms ≈ 5 frames, so timing resolution is ±33 ms. 60 fps gives ±17 ms, and 240 fps gives ±4 ms.
- **Sequencing:** pelvis-to-torso peak gaps in skilled baseball hitters are only ~5 ms median (IQR −8 to +14 ms), and 39% of skilled swings showed the torso peaking first. A camera app should not flag "out of sequence" from pelvis/torso peak order at phone frame rates. It is below resolution and often "wrong" even in good hitters.
- **Slow-pitch bat speed** for skilled adult men is ~66–70 mph at contact (Wu et al.), comparable to college fastpitch (58–70) and the OBP college baseball sample (68 mph Blast). Lower-body velocity ranges similar to baseball are plausible for strong slow-pitch hitters, but this is unverified.

### Gaps
- **No published slow-pitch lower-body benchmarks** were found: no stride length, pelvis velocity, pelvis angle at contact, lead-knee, COM or phase-timing data for slow-pitch. This is the biggest gap. The Alberta thesis may contain stride-technique kinematics but was not read in full.
- Blast does not appear to publish slowpitch-specific TTC or bat-speed norms. Its softball norms are fastpitch-based.
- Welch 1995 stride length and timing values (full text) were not retrieved. Only the abstract's 714 / 937 deg/s and 123% BW figures were confirmed.
- Escamilla 2009 pelvis velocity and stride values (full text, behind a 403) were not retrieved.
- Fortenbaugh's dissertation ("The Biomechanics of the Baseball Swing", U. Miami 2011) phase definitions and timing values were not retrieved.
- Pro-vs-amateur pelvis velocity comparisons from a single consistent system (e.g., K-Vest population norms) were not found publicly. The OBP sample is mostly college and shows little level effect.
- All OBP-derived numbers are from machine pitching at ~65 mph from ~40 ft (perceived velocity differs from game pitching), so timing values especially may not transfer.

## 4. Public datasets (Driveline OBP, Reboot): which lower-body variables and summary statistics they release

### Takeaway
Driveline's OpenBiomechanics hitting release is the only substantial public 3D hitting dataset found. It contains a POI table (677 swings, 98 hitters) with pelvis, torso and X-factor angles and velocities at first move / heel strike / foot plant, lead-knee and rear-hip angles, and max COG velocity. It also has full-signal tables (360 Hz joint centers including ankles, knees, hips and whole-body COM; joint angles and velocities; 1080 Hz force plates) and event times (10% and 100% BW front-foot contact, ball contact). The README releases variable definitions but **no numeric summary statistics**. All statistics in this file were computed here from the raw data. Reboot publishes definitions but no public hitting dataset was found.

### Cited Findings
- OBP hitting contents: C3D files (with static trials), full-signal CSVs (`force_plate`, `joint_angles`, `joint_velos`, `landmarks`) joined on `session_swing` + `time`, `metadata.csv`, `poi_metrics.csv`, and `hittrax.csv`. Full-signal data and C3Ds are release assets under tag `dataset-v1`. — [OBP hitting README](https://github.com/drivelineresearch/openbiomechanics/tree/main/baseball_hitting); [OBP release dataset-v1](https://github.com/drivelineresearch/openbiomechanics/releases/tag/dataset-v1)

- Derived-metric methods used in this file (all on OBP `landmarks` at 360 Hz; lead leg = left for R hitters; y mirrored for L hitters so + = toward the plate):
  - *Stride* = horizontal (x-y) distance between lead and rear ankle joint centers at `fp_10_time`.
  - *Leg length* = mean thigh + shank length over the first 50 frames.
  - *Stance width* = ankle distance in the first frame.
  - *Lead-foot lift* = first frame where lead-ankle z exceeds its median stance height + 0.02 m.
  - *Pelvis angle* = atan2 of the rear-hip → lead-hip vector in the horizontal plane (0 = pointing at the pitcher, + = open). *Shoulder angle* is the same using shoulder joint centers. *Camera-style separation* = pelvis − shoulder angle.

  - *COM travel* = `centerofmass_x` change / height.
  - *Pelvis/torso peaks and timing* from `joint_velos` `pelvis_angular_velocity_z` and `torso_angular_velocity_z` within [foot contact − 0.5 s, contact + 0.01 s].

### Inferences
- OBP's `landmarks` table has the same kind of data as MediaPipe Pose output: joint centers for hips, knees, ankles, shoulders, wrists, plus COM. It is a good offline reference for checking the app's lower-body metric code. The app's stride / knee / pelvis-angle formulas can be run on OBP landmarks to check they reproduce the distributions above before applying them to phone video. The license bars commercial use, including by sole proprietors who intend to profit "now or in the future". If Gapper is or may become a commercial product, using OBP data even internally for threshold-setting may need a commercial license from Driveline. Get that decision before relying on these derived numbers in product code. The published-literature numbers (Welch, Escamilla, Blast, etc.) carry no such restriction.
- Because OBP has no slow-pitch swings, a slow-pitch-specific reference set would need to be collected (e.g., the app's own opt-in data, or 240 fps phone captures of skilled slow-pitch hitters).

- No public Reboot Motion hitting dataset or published Reboot hitting summary statistics were found.
- Exact OBP definitions of "first move", "heel strike", "load/loaded position" and "max hip-shoulder separation" are not documented in the README.
- No public slow-pitch or fastpitch softball 3D dataset was found.
